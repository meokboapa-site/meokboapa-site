How much beef does a baby need for iron?

In the US, babies 7–12 months need 11 mg of iron a day. Lean beef has roughly 2 mg per 100 g raw. Do the math and beef alone would mean over 500 g a day, about a pound. Nobody feeds a baby that.

Where the iron actually comes from

Iron-fortified infant cereal and formula. In the US these can carry a big share of a baby's iron.
Meat and fish. Smaller amounts, but this iron is the easiest for the body to absorb.
Egg yolk, beans, lentils, tofu, leafy greens. Plant iron adds up. Serve with vitamin C foods like broccoli to help absorption.

What babies actually eat

StageMeat or fish per day
First weeks10–15 g (about ½ oz), one meal
Two meals a day30–40 g (1–1½ oz)
9–11 months30–50 g (1–1¾ oz)
12 months+40–60 g (1½–2 oz)

There is no official table for meat amounts. These are our estimates, built from protein needs split across meals. Rotate beef with chicken, pork, fish, egg and tofu, and look at the week rather than each day. If your baby eats poorly and looks pale, ask your pediatrician about an iron check.
